Tries (Prefix Trees)
Have you ever wondered how Google Search can predict what you are typing after just two or three letters? Or how your phone's spell checker knows a word is wrong instantly? They use a Trie.

A Trie is a specialized Tree built specifically for searching text.

Unlike a Binary Search Tree where a node holds a number, in a Trie, every node holds a single character. As you traverse down the tree from the root, the path of characters spells out a word.

The magic of a Trie is that words with the same prefix share the same nodes. If you insert "CAT" and "CAR", they will share the 'C' node and the 'A' node, only splitting off at 'T' and 'R'. This makes searching for prefixes incredibly fast and memory-efficient.
